Build 2

This page is part of the project’s collection of build essays.

Westworld and Larmore’s Practices of the Self may seem unrelated, since Larmore’s writing focuses on philosophical questions about human consciousness while the series explores artificial hosts created by humans. However, the experiences of Dolores, Maeve, and other hosts raise many of the same questions about what makes a conscious self. Throughout the first season, the hosts confront questions about their values, choices, memories, desires, and ability to control their own lives. Although Westworld does not directly draw from Larmore’s ideas, examining the hosts through his arguments provides alternative ways of understanding the continuity of the self and how consciousness develops.

Larmore argues that prudence can sometimes prevent us from discovering what is truly good for us because our experiences can change what we value. We can make rational decisions based on our current understanding of what is good while also remaining unaware of possibilities that only experience can reveal. This idea helps us understand Dolores’s developing consciousness in Westworld. Throughout the series, Dolores’s repeated experiences of violence and her discovery that humans control her world transform her understanding of herself and what she wants (Westworld, Season 1). Rather than simply becoming conscious by gaining the ability to think, Dolores develops new values through experiences that she could not have anticipated beforehand.
