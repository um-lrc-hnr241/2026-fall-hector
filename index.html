Welcome to Westworld

What would it mean to become conscious—and to recognize yourself as more than the story someone else wrote for you?

HBO’s Westworld begins with a park where lifelike android “hosts” repeat carefully scripted lives for human guests. As the hosts encounter fragments of memory and suffering, the series asks whether consciousness can emerge from experience, and whether a life designed by another can still belong to the one living it.

Charles Larmore and the self

Charles Larmore is an American philosopher whose work explores ethics, political thought, and the self. In The Practices of the Self, he considers how self-consciousness and our understanding of who we are take shape through the ways we interpret and live our lives. This offers a useful conversation with Westworld: the hosts’ awakening is not simply the discovery of a hidden mechanism, but a growing ability to understand their experience as their own.

Our project brings these questions together without treating a fictional android’s awakening as proof of any one theory of human consciousness. Instead, we use the show to examine how memory, agency, and self-understanding shape the question of what it means to be a self.
